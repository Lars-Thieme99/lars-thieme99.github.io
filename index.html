Research

Work in progress

Competition in information design with costly information

Abstract

We study competition in information design when information is costly. Two senders with opposed preferences each commit to a costly experiment to influence a receiver's action. We show that no informative pure-strategy equilibrium survives in finite signal spaces and characterize the unique symmetric equilibrium in closed form. In it, neither sender profits from the information he provides: each chooses an experiment that leaves his opponent indifferent to providing any, so competition is pure expenditure for the senders and pure gain for the receiver. As information becomes more expensive, each sender's experiment, and hence the joint experiment the receiver observes, becomes Blackwell less informative. Competition thus benefits the receiver, but decreasingly so as information costs increase.
